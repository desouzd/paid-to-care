Doulas: getting paid by MassHealth

Since December 8, 2023, MassHealth pays doulas who are enrolled as MassHealth providers. Here's how to get enrolled.

$1,065MassHealth rate for up to 8 visits plus labor support
~2 hoursRequired free MassHealth doula training
18+Minimum age for in-state doulas
  1. An NPI is your 10-digit health-provider ID. Apply online for an individual (Type 1) NPI through NPPES. It's free and usually takes a few days.
    Apply at NPPES →
  2. A free online course, about 2 hours. Save the completion certificate — you must include it with your application.
    Open the training → · Instructions
  3. You need one of these:
    • A certificate of completion from a doula training, or a letter from a doula certification organization, plus a signed attestation form saying the training covered the required skills; or
    • Recommendations from 3 former clients (paid or volunteer) and 2 licensed health care providers (e.g. midwife, nurse, physician, social worker) who saw your work — all within the last 5 years.
    Doula certification checklist →
  4. Use the official checklist so nothing is missing. Solo doulas use the individual checklist; doulas billing together use the group practice one.
    Individual checklist → · Group checklist · MassHealth Doula Initiative
  5. Send everything to MassHealth Provider Enrollment and Credentialing:
    • Fax: (617) 988-8974
    • Mail: PO Box 278, Quincy, MA 02171-0278
    Keep a copy of everything you send and write down the date.
  6. MassHealth only pays for services on dates when you're already an enrolled provider — so don't start billable visits until you're approved.
  7. Many MassHealth members are in a managed care plan (an ACO or MCO). These plans may have their own sign-up steps for doulas. Ask each plan your clients use how to join their network.

Rules can change. This guide summarizes official MassHealth materials as of October 2026 — always confirm with MassHealth before submitting.

Recovery coaches: certification, the new license, and pay

Massachusetts passed a law (Chapter 285 of the Acts of 2024, signed December 23, 2024) that creates a state license for recovery coaches and requires insurers to cover recovery coaching.

Jan 1, 2026All insured health plans must cover recovery coaching
$0Copay for clients — no cost-sharing or prior authorization
60 + 500 hrsIn-person training plus supervised work for the CARC
  1. The CCAR Recovery Coach Academy is the standard starting training in Massachusetts and is offered by several organizations around the state.
    Find trainings (MassRecovery) →
  2. The Certified Addiction Recovery Coach (CARC) credential comes from the Massachusetts Board of Substance Abuse Counselor Certification (MBSACC). You need:
    • Personal experience with addiction and recovery — recovery coaching is a peer role. Expect to need at least 1 year of sustained recovery (MassHealth's peer recovery coach role requires 2 years).
    • A high school diploma or GED
    • 60 hours of in-person education across advocacy, mentoring, recovery/wellness support and ethics (online courses don't count for first-time certification)
    • 500 hours of supervised work, paid or volunteer
    • Living or working in Massachusetts at least 51% of the time
    • A hand-written application — typed applications are returned
    Career path overview →
  3. The 2024 law moves recovery coach licensing to the Department of Public Health (DPH). Once DPH's licensing process is live, you'll likely need the license to use the "recovery coach" title and to be paid by insurers.
    Read Chapter 285 → · Recovery Coach Commission
    • MassHealth covers recovery coach services, mostly billed by licensed treatment programs and organizations that employ coaches. MassHealth's "peer recovery coach" role requires at least 2 years of sustained recovery.
    • Private insurance must cover recovery coaching for plans starting or renewing on or after January 1, 2026, at rates no lower than MassHealth's — creating new paid positions.
    In practice, most coaches are paid as employees of programs, hospitals or community organizations rather than billing insurance on their own.
  5. Look at treatment programs, emergency departments, community health centers and recovery community centers — the new insurance requirement means more of them can afford to hire coaches.
    Careers of Substance job resources →

The licensing and insurance rules are new and still being put in place. Confirm details with DPH, MBSACC and your employer.
