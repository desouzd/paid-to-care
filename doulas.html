Doulas: getting paid by MassHealth

Since December 8, 2023, MassHealth pays doulas who are enrolled as MassHealth providers. Here's how to get enrolled.

Up to ~$1,700Per client: about $800 for up to 8 hours of visits + $900 for birth support
~2 hoursRequired free MassHealth doula training
18+Minimum age for in-state doulas
New: free state doula certification (optional)

In 2026 the Department of Public Health launched a free, voluntary doula certification. You don't need it to work as a doula, and it's a separate process from enrolling with MassHealth — but being a MassHealth provider is one of the ways to qualify, and it may matter if private insurers start covering doulas (a bill to require that is moving through the Legislature). Learn about DPH doula certification →

Part 1

Start here: steps only you can do

These are tied to your identity, so the law requires you to do them yourself. They take about 2–3 hours total.

  1. An NPI is your 10-digit health-provider ID. Create your own account and apply for an individual (Type 1) NPI through NPPES. It's free and usually takes a few days.
    Apply at NPPES →
  2. A free online course, about 2 hours. It has to be you who takes it. Download and save the completion certificate — it goes in your application.
    Open the training → · Instructions
Part 2

Next steps: do them yourself, or let us do them

Everything below is paperwork and follow-up. Use the steps to do it on your own, or hand it to us — you'll only need to review and sign.

  1. You need one of these:
    • A certificate from a doula training, or a letter from a doula certification organization, plus an attestation form (signed by you) saying the training covered the required skills; or
    • Recommendations from 3 former clients (paid or volunteer) and 2 licensed health care providers (e.g. midwife, nurse, physician, social worker) who saw your work — all within the last 5 years.
    We can request and collect the recommendation letters for you and check everything is complete.
    Doula certification checklist → Have us do this →
  2. Solo doulas use the individual checklist; doulas billing together use the group practice one. We can fill it out from your information — you review it and sign.
    Individual checklist → · Group checklist · MassHealth Doula Initiative Have us do this →
  3. Send everything to MassHealth Provider Enrollment and Credentialing:
    • Fax: (617) 988-8974
    • Mail: PO Box 278, Quincy, MA 02171-0278
    Keep a copy of everything and write down the date. We can send it for you once you've signed. Have us do this →
  4. MassHealth only pays for visits on dates when you're already enrolled, so don't start billable visits until you're approved. We can follow up on your application and tell you when it's through. Have us do this →
  5. Many MassHealth members are in a managed care plan (an ACO or MCO), and these plans may have their own sign-up steps. We can find out what each plan needs and handle the paperwork. Have us do this →

Rules can change. This guide summarizes official MassHealth materials as of October 2026 — always confirm with MassHealth before submitting.
