Doulas: getting paid by MassHealth

Since December 8, 2023, MassHealth pays doulas who are enrolled as MassHealth providers. Here's how to get enrolled.

~$1,700Per client, at most: about $800 for visits plus $900 for birth support
~2 hoursRequired free MassHealth doula training
18+Minimum age for in-state doulas
New: free state doula certification (optional)

In 2026 the Department of Public Health launched a free, voluntary doula certification. You don't need it to work as a doula, and it's a separate process from enrolling with MassHealth. Being a MassHealth provider is one of the ways to qualify, and it may matter if private insurers start covering doulas (a bill to require that is moving through the Legislature). Part 1

Start here: steps only you can do

These are tied to your identity, so the law requires you to do them yourself. They take about 2–3 hours total.

  1. An NPI is your 10-digit health-provider ID. Create your own account and apply for an individual (Type 1) NPI through NPPES. It's free and usually takes a few days.
    Apply for your NPI →
  2. A free online course, about 2 hours. It has to be you who takes it. Download and save the completion certificate. It goes in your application.
    Walk me through the training →Went to a live MassHealth session instead? See your options
Part 2

Next steps: the paperwork

Everything below is forms and follow-up. Take it one step at a time. Each step links to the official source.

  1. You need one of three things: a certificate from a doula training, a letter from a doula certification organization, or recommendations from past clients and health care providers. Tip: if you go the recommendation route, ask early, since people often take a few weeks to reply.
    Compare your three options →
  2. Have your NPI number, training certificate and proof of training ready before you start.
    Open the application checklist →Billing with other doulas? Use the group practice checklist instead.
  3. Send everything to MassHealth Provider Enrollment and Credentialing:
    • Fax: (617) 988-8974
    • Mail: PO Box 278, Quincy, MA 02171-0278
    Keep a copy of everything and write down the date.
  4. MassHealth only pays for visits on dates when you're already enrolled, so don't start billable visits until you're approved. If you haven't heard back in a few weeks, call MassHealth Customer Service at (800) 841-2900 to check on it.
  5. Many MassHealth members are in a managed care plan (an ACO or MCO), and these plans may have their own sign-up steps. Ask each plan your clients use how to join their network.

Rules can change. This guide summarizes official MassHealth materials as of October 2026. Always confirm with MassHealth before submitting.
