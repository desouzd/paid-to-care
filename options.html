Training and certification: every option

"Certified" can mean three different things for a Massachusetts doula. Two are required to get paid by MassHealth, and one is optional. Here's each one, with every way to meet it.

Quick answer: what do I need?

To get paid by MassHealth: you need 1. MassHealth's training and 2. proof of your doula training.
Optional: 3. the state's DPH doula certification. It's free and separate from MassHealth.

Required for MassHealth

1. MassHealth's doula provider training

Everyone who enrolls with MassHealth has to complete MassHealth's own training about the program. Choose the option that fits you:

Option A: Take the free online course

About 2 hours of video on MassHealth's training website. When you finish, you download a certificate for your application.

Our click-by-click walkthrough → · Training sign-up page

Option B: You attended a live MassHealth session

If you attended one of MassHealth's live doula training sessions from start to finish, you've already met this requirement and don't need to send a certificate.

MassHealth's training instructions

Required for MassHealth

2. Proof of your doula training

MassHealth also needs proof that you're trained as a doula. Pick one of these three options. MassHealth doesn't keep a list of approved training programs, but your training has to cover the required skills, such as pregnancy and birth basics, common medical interventions and complications, labor comfort measures and basic newborn care.

Option A: A doula training program certificate

Your certificate of completion from a doula training, plus MassHealth's attestation form (you sign it to confirm the training covered the required skills).

Some Massachusetts trainings: New England Community Doula Academy · SMC Full Circle Doula · Birth Coach Method · Boston Public Health Commission (community doula training)

Option B: A doula certification organization

Proof of certification, or a letter, from a doula certifying organization, plus the same signed attestation form.

Examples: DONA International · CAPPA

Option C: Your experience

No formal training? Use recommendations instead: 3 former clients (paid or volunteer) and 2 licensed health care providers (such as a midwife, nurse, physician or social worker) who saw your work, all within the last 5 years.

Tip: ask early. Recommendations can take a few weeks to come back.

Optional

3. The state's DPH doula certification

The Department of Public Health offers a free, voluntary doula certification. You don't need it to work as a doula or to get paid by MassHealth, and you apply for it separately. It may matter more if private insurers start covering doulas. Choose one pathway:

Experience pathway

3 client recommendation forms and 2 provider recommendation forms.

Training pathway

Your certificate of completion from a doula training program.

MassHealth pathway

Proof you're a MassHealth doula provider, such as your welcome letter, a MassHealth portal screenshot or the provider enrollment status lookup.

Reciprocity pathway

Your doula certification from New York or Rhode Island.

Everyone applying also needs: to be 18 or older, a headshot, a copy of a government ID, a signed and notarized CORI (background check) acknowledgement form, the MassHealth doula training or DPH's on-demand MassHealth webinar, and to agree to the Doula Code of Conduct.

How to apply: online through DPH's Health Professions Licensing System. Choose "Apply for a New License," search for "Doula," then select "Doula Certification."
